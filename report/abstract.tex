\begin{abstract}
Single-trial classification of graded upper-limb movements from scalp EEG remains difficult because of low signal-to-noise ratio, spatial smearing due to volume conduction, and high inter-trial variability.
This work systematically asks whether engineered spectral summaries and pairwise oscillatory coupling---in addition to a strong temporal convolutional baseline---raise multiclass accuracy on publicly available movement-execution EEG~\cite{ofner2017upper}.

We compare a Common Spatial Patterns (CSP) plus Linear Discriminant Analysis (LDA) pipeline, an EEGNet-style convolutional model~\cite{lawhern2018eegnet}, and two dual-branch networks that fuse raw waveforms with either Welch power spectral density~(PSD) maps or magnitude features from wavelet complex cross-spectral density at motor-relevant frequencies (10 and 22\,Hz in our implementation).

Training followed the repository preprocessing and optimisation scripts (\(\approx\)85/15 stratified split, subjects pooled).

Held-out accuracy increased from CSP (\(\sim\)38\%), to EEGNet (\(\sim\)56\%), to PSD fusion (\(\sim\)61\%), and to connectivity attention fusion (\(\sim\)61--62\%), so multimodal fusion adds only a modest increment beyond the temporal branch.
Connectivity topographies contrasting hand-related movement epochs with rest nevertheless retain structured spatial contrast---evidence that ancillary features carry neurophysiologically interpretable information despite the limited classification gains.
Finally, two-dimensional t-SNE embeddings of penultimate activations show persistent class overlap across architectures, aligning with an optimisation narrative in which variance-rich temporal pathways absorb most of the learning signal.
\end{abstract}
