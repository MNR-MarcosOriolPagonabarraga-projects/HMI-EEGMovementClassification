\begin{abstract}
Decoding complex upper-limb motor intent (Rest, Elbow, Hand, Forearm) from non-invasive scalp EEG remains a formidable challenge due to volume conduction and severe single-trial noise.
In this report, we systematically evaluate a progression of decoding strategies, advancing from classical Common Spatial Patterns (CSP) to deep convolutional baselines (EEGNet).
To break a stubborn generalization plateau, we introduce multi-modal dual-branch architectures fusing raw temporal epochs with frequency-domain features, including Welch Power Spectral Density (PSD) and wavelet-based Cross-Spectral Density (CSD)~\cite{gramfort2013meg,ofner2017upper}.
Crucially, our quantitative analysis uncovers a ``dual-branch dilemma'': high-capacity temporal convolutions aggressively overfit to single-trial noise, actively suppressing the highly discriminative power of the connectivity features.
Ultimately, we demonstrate that projecting functional connectivity matrices onto a Riemannian Tangent Space and extracting topological Node Degrees overcomes this bottleneck, offering a highly-regularized, geometrically sound framework for robust motor decoding.
\end{abstract}
