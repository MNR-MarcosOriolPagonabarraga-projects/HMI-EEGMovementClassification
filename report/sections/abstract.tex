\begin{abstract}
Classifying electroencephalogram (EEG) signals for motor imagery or execution is a foundational task in Human-Machine Interfaces (HMI). While the Common Spatial Pattern (CSP) algorithm coupled with Linear Discriminant Analysis (LDA) is highly effective for binary inter-limb tasks (e.g., left vs. right hand), its performance degrades significantly when classifying different movements originating from the same limb. This paper investigates the limitations of the traditional CSP-LDA pipeline for intra-limb movement classification and proposes three deep learning architectures—EEGNet, EEGPsdNet, and EEGConnNet—to overcome these shortcomings. By analyzing latent space representations and classification outcomes, we demonstrate the necessity and efficacy of deep neural networks in extracting complex spatial-spectral features for high-resolution EEG movement classification.
\end{abstract}
