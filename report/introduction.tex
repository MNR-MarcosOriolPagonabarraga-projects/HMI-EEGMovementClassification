\section{Introduction}

\label{sec:intro}

Decoding multiple upper-limb movement states from scalp EEG motivates brain--computer interfaces, neurorehabilitation, and robotic assistive devices~\cite{ofner2017upper}.
Signal quality at the scalp is limited by spatial mixing (volume conduction), low SNR, and variability between trials, which complicates multiclass decoding.

\textbf{Purpose of this work.}
We organise the approach as two blocks applied to identical cue-locked data.
\textbf{Block~I} is a classical \emph{CSP+LDA pipeline}: handcrafted spatial filters optimised for discriminative variance, followed by a linear Gaussian classifier~\cite{gramfort2013meg}.

\textbf{Block~II} is a set of \emph{convolutional decoders}: an EEGNet-style architecture~\cite{lawhern2018eegnet} operating on raw voltage epochs together with two custom dual-branch models that concatenate or softly gate auxiliary tensors derived from Welch spectral maps and pairwise wavelet cross-spectral features.

\textbf{Contribution.}
The comparison isolates where accuracy gains originate when moving from linear spatial prototypes to differentiable temporal receptive fields and, subsequently, multimodal fusion.
Section~\ref{sec:methods} lays out methodology in that order~(CSP, then convolutional architectures); Section~\ref{sec:results} reports withheld accuracy and accompanying figures; Section~\ref{sec:discussion} interprets them.
