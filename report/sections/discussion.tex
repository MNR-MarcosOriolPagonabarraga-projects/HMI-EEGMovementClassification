\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The classification of intra-limb movements presents a severe challenge due to the heavily overlapping cortical activation patterns within the same hemisphere. As demonstrated in Table~\ref{tab:main}, the classical exploratory CSP pipeline hovered near chance-level accuracy ($\sim$36\%) for this four-class task. 
The transition to convolutional receptive fields (Block II) yielded an immediate improvement of approximately 20 percentage points over the optimized shallow CSP baseline. 
This substantial leap suggests that successful same-limb decoding relies heavily on non-linear spatio-temporal dynamics that simple spatial variance filters inherently fail to capture.

\textbf{The Efficacy and Boundaries of Multimodal Fusion.}
While deep temporal filters began untangling the latent manifolds, synergistic movements (e.g., hand versus forearm) still suffered from significant misclassification bottlenecks. 
Integrating multimodal priors successfully addressed this. Welch PSD concatenation and cross-spectral coherence attention each added roughly 5 percentage points of accuracy over the EEGNet-style baseline. 
The $t$-SNE embeddings in Figure~\ref{fig:master_grid} however do not show much clearer clusters in the fusion approaches.  

\textbf{Attention gates.}
While Table~\ref{tab:main} shows only modest incremental accuracy gains from connectivity fusion, 
Figure~\ref{fig:connTopo} shows that the connectivity network organizes clusters differently than the other multimodal approaches, effectively separating the elbow class from the hand and forearm. We suspect that the attention gate holds better capabilities for motor limbs decoding for EEG.
